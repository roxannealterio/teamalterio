The market

Who buys here, and why.

Holland Park West does something unusual for a suburb this close to the city. The blocks are generous by inner south standards, much of the housing sits high enough to hold a view, and both the motorway and the busway are minutes away.

The buyers are mainly families moving up from Greenslopes and Coorparoo in search of a fourth bedroom and a yard, alongside downsizers who want to stay close to family and schools. Both groups know the recent sales well, which is why an overpriced home sits here.

The practical stuff

Getting around, and getting in.

  • Getting in and out. Holland Park West busway station on the South East Busway, with the Pacific Motorway along the western edge
  • Schools in the suburb. Marshall Road State School, Holland Park State High School and Nursery Road State Special School
  • Neighbours. Greenslopes, Holland Park, Mount Gravatt East, Tarragindi, Nathan and Mount Gravatt
  • Green space. Whites Hill Reserve is a short drive, with bushland tracks, sporting fields and lookouts across the city
  • Local history. The Holland Park Hotel, Glindemann Farmhouse, a former tram shelter and St Joachim's Catholic Church are all heritage-listed

Living here

Why people stay.

01

Close in, without the squeeze

Eight kilometres from the CBD, and yet the blocks remain generous for that distance. You get a yard, a driveway you can turn a car around in, and room for the trampoline. Two suburbs closer to town, the same money buys a townhouse.

02

Two ways into town

Holland Park West busway station puts you on the South East Busway with no traffic between you and the city, while the Pacific Motorway runs along the western edge for anyone who would rather drive.

03

Height, and the view that comes with it

Much of the suburb sits high, and the right block will catch the city skyline to the north west along with a breeze in February.

04

Schools inside the boundary

Marshall Road State School, Holland Park State High School and Nursery Road State Special School all sit inside the suburb boundary, with Griffith University's Nathan campus a short drive away.

05

Everything within ten minutes

Greenslopes Mall and Westfield Garden City for the shopping, Greenslopes Private Hospital for the part nobody plans for, and the cafes and restaurants along Logan Road for everything else.

06

Green space on the doorstep

Whites Hill Reserve offers bushland tracks, sporting fields and lookouts across the city, and the Mount Gravatt lookout is only minutes away.

07

A suburb that is being rebuilt

Post war houses on good blocks are steadily being replaced by new homes, which is why the street you buy into today will look quite different in ten years. It also means land here has a second buyer: the one who wants the block rather than the house.

Street to street

What actually changes the price.

Two houses with the same floorplan can sit $100,000 apart in this suburb. These are usually the reasons why.

Elevation

Height is the single biggest swing factor here. A block that catches the skyline to the north west will sell well above one sitting in a gully two streets away.

Motorway noise

The M3 cuts both ways. It is excellent for the commute and audible along the western edge, and buyers price that in. Where the house sits relative to the cutting matters far more than the distance on a map.

Catchment

Families buying for a particular school will pay to be on the right side of the line, and they will not consider anything outside it. Always worth confirming before you go to market.

Block shape and access

Side access, a flat rear yard and a driveway you can turn a car around in are worth more in this suburb than an extra bathroom.

What's underneath

A great deal of post war stock has been lifted and built in underneath. Whether that work was approved and certified makes a real difference now that the disclosure rules have tightened.
