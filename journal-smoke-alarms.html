Every Queensland home needs interconnected smoke alarms by 1 January 2027

2 October 2026 · Roxanne Alterio

If you own the home you live in, Queensland's smoke alarm rules change on 1 January 2027. From that date all owner-occupied private homes, townhouses and units have to meet the same standard that rental and recently sold properties have had to meet for years.

What the standard actually is

Three things have to be true of the alarms in the house:

  • They are photoelectric, the type that reacts to smouldering smoke rather than flame
  • They are interconnected, so when one sounds, every alarm in the house sounds
  • They meet Australian Standard 3786-2014

Interconnection is the part people underestimate. A working alarm in a hallway is no use to someone asleep at the other end of the house with the door shut. When they are linked, the whole house gets the same warning at the same time.

Selling or renting out a home? You already have to comply at the point of sale or lease. That rule started in 2022. The 2027 date simply closes the gap for everyone else.

What this means if you're selling

Compliance comes up twice in a sale. It is a question on the paperwork, and it is one of the first things a building and pest inspector notes. A buyer will not walk away over the alarms, but they will use them to chip at the price late in the piece.

It is cheap to deal with early. Electricians are quoting a few hundred dollars for a typical house, and the work takes a morning. Compare that to a buyer asking for a reduction two days before settlement.

Worth doing now, not in December

Electricians get busy as the deadline closes in. If your alarms are the old ionisation type, or they are not linked, get a quote now while you can still choose when the work happens.

Quick self-check

  • Look at the alarm. Photoelectric units usually say so on the housing
  • Check the date stamped on it. Alarms expire about ten years from manufacture
  • Press and hold the test button on one. If the others don't sound too, they are not interconnected
  • Count the bedrooms. You need an alarm in each bedroom, in hallways connecting bedrooms, and on every storey
