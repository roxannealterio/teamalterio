Transfer duty concessions now depend on your residency status

12 September 2026 · Roxanne Alterio

From 1 August 2026, Queensland's transfer duty home concessions are restricted by residency status. The concession now applies only where the buyer is:

  • an Australian citizen, or
  • a permanent resident, or
  • a self-funded foreign retiree on a legacy subclass 405 or 410 visa

It applies to contracts signed on or after that date, and it covers the home concession, the first home concession and the first home vacant land concession.

What changes for a temporary visa holder

Two things happen at once. The concessional rate is gone, so duty is calculated at the standard rates. On top of that, additional foreign acquirer duty of 8% applies.

On a $1.25 million home, that's the difference between a concessional calculation and full duty plus an 8% surcharge. That can change which suburb you can afford, so work it out before you fall in love with a house.

If you're buying

Work out your duty position before you start inspecting, not after you've made an offer. A broker and a solicitor can tell you in an afternoon where you actually sit, and whether the date you sign matters. If your permanent residency is close to being granted, timing could be worth real money.

If you're selling

In suburbs with a lot of temporary residents this changes who can bid. There are not fewer buyers, but the ones affected have less to spend and will push harder on price and conditions. Knowing which of your buyers this touches is useful when you're weighing up offers.

The Queensland Revenue Office publishes the detail and a duty calculator, and your solicitor can confirm your position. Don't take a number from a portal's estimator as gospel.
